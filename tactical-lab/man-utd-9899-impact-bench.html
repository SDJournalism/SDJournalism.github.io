The Impact Bench

No English club has won the treble since. Manchester United's 1998/99 season is remembered for two substitutes scoring twice in two minutes in Barcelona, but the foundation for that moment was built months earlier, in a squad deep enough to survive three competitions running at once.

A strike partnership that shared everything

Dwight Yorke and Andy Cole scored 53 goals between them across all competitions that season, with Yorke reaching 18 in the league and Cole 17 -- the pairing clicking fully from a 6-2 win over Leicester in January 1999, when Yorke's hat-trick and Cole's double turned an already good partnership into an unstoppable one. Gary Neville later said of their form in Europe: "They lit up the whole league, they lit up Europe. I've never seen anything like it" (Sky Sports, 2026). What made it work wasn't just quality -- it was a total absence of ego: "They didn't mind which of them scored... they got to the point when they didn't care if either of them scored, they'd both be happy" (Sky Sports, 2026).

Depth as a weapon, not an afterthought

Sir Alex Ferguson rotated squad players through a fixture list that ran across the Premier League, FA Cup and Champions League simultaneously, and the bench kept producing when it mattered -- Ole Gunnar Solskjaer once came on to score four goals in twelve minutes against Nottingham Forest from the substitutes' bench alone (Premier League, 2026). Dwight Yorke topped the league's scoring charts with 18 goals, level with Michael Owen and Jimmy Floyd Hasselbaink, as United won the title by a single point from Arsenal (Premier League, 2026).

Two minutes in Barcelona

The treble came down to the Champions League final against Bayern Munich, still 0-1 down deep into stoppage time until two substitutes changed the outcome: Teddy Sheringham equalised in the 91st minute from a David Beckham corner, then teed up Ole Gunnar Solskjaer's winner two minutes later with an identical flicked header (BBC Sport, 2026). "I remember the celebration when I slid and then all these bodies piling on top of me... I could hear it but I wasn't there," Solskjaer said of the moment. He put the whole season down to a culture set from the top: "It came from the top. The manager instilled this hunger and humility, a desire to work hard and win" (BBC Sport, 2026).

A record that has stood for 27 years

Manchester City have come closest to matching it since, but as of 2026 no other English club has completed the treble. It remains the clearest evidence in Premier League history that a squad, not just a starting eleven, wins competitions fought on three fronts at once.
