The Centurions

No team before or since has reached 100 points in a single English top-flight season. Manchester City's 2017/18 side didn't just win the title -- they won it while sat at home, sealed by a result going on somewhere else entirely.

The false full-back

Pep Guardiola spent more than £100m rebuilding his full-backs specifically so they could do something other full-backs at the time weren't asked to do. Kyle Walker, Benjamin Mendy, Danilo and Fabian Delph would invert into midfield once City had the ball, turning a back four into a back three in possession and freeing an extra central body to play alongside Fernandinho -- a mechanism that felt genuinely new to an English top flight still built largely around orthodox wing-backs (The Football Faithful, 2026).

Five up, five back

Guardiola described the resulting shape as a five-five split: five players committed to attack, five holding defensive shape, at every moment of the game. David Silva and Kevin De Bruyne dropped short to combine centrally and progress the ball through pressure, while Leroy Sane and Raheem Sterling stayed high and wide to stretch defences and create the one-on-one space that Sergio Aguero thrived on (The Football Faithful, 2026).

A goalkeeper who could pass

Underneath all of it was a goalkeeper who could pass. Ederson's distribution was the piece that let the whole structure work at real speed -- accurate enough over long range to bypass an entire press in a single ball, and comfortable enough in possession to support an extremely high defensive line that would have been reckless behind almost any other goalkeeper in the league at the time.

The numbers

The final tally still reads like a misprint: 100 points from 32 wins, 4 draws and just 2 defeats all season, with 106 goals scored, 27 conceded and a goal difference of plus 79 (Wikipedia, 2026). The title was confirmed on 15 April 2018, with five matches still to play, the moment Manchester United lost 1-0 away at West Bromwich Albion (Man City, 2026).
