The Shift and Shoot

Cole Palmer's finishing doesn't look rushed, even in the split-second before it happens -- and that's the whole trick. The technique that broke Chelsea's rookie scoring record starts with a shift of the hips, not a burst of pace.

The shift and shoot

Instead of shooting first-time, Palmer takes an extra half-touch to move the ball across his own body and his marker's, opening up the far corner before the defender or goalkeeper can react. It sacrifices power for disguise, and it works because he can finish comfortably off either side once the space appears (Coaches' Voice, 2026).

Built to receive on the half-turn

The technique only works because of what happens before it. Palmer is comfortable receiving on the half-turn in the inside-right channel, already facing goal before a marker can close him down, and his passing range -- particularly bending through balls into the gap between full-back and centre-back -- means defenders can never fully commit to standing goal-side of him (Coaches' Voice, 2026).

A record-breaking debut season

That combination produced one of the great debut seasons in Chelsea history: 22 goals and 11 assists in 2023/24, making him the first Chelsea player aged 21 or under to reach 10 Premier League goals in a season and only the third to score 20 in a debut campaign, after Jimmy Floyd Hasselbaink and Diego Costa (Wikipedia, 2026).

A tougher second act

His follow-up season was more stop-start -- 15 goals in 2024/25, including a run of 18 games and over 1,000 minutes without scoring -- a reminder that a technique built on disguise and timing is harder to sustain when a team's overall rhythm dips. The shift-and-shoot itself, though, hasn't gone anywhere; Chelsea's whole approach in the final third is still built around getting him the ball facing forward, in that same inside-right pocket, as often as possible.
