SubjectLeicester City, 2015/16
ClubLeicester City
CompetitionPremier League

The Counter-Attack Kings

Leicester City went into the 2015-16 season as favourites for relegation, having survived the previous campaign only by winning seven of their last nine games under Nigel Pearson. Bookmakers priced them at 5000-1 to win the Premier League. Ten months later, Claudio Ranieri's side were champions -- the first title in the club's 132-year history, and still English football's most improbable.

The tactical reset

Ranieri's first major decision was to abandon the back three that had rescued Leicester the previous spring, switching instead to a compact 4-4-2 that became a 4-4-1-1 without the ball. Leicester used 11 different formations the year before Ranieri arrived; in 2015-16, they stuck with this same shape in 35 of their 38 matches (Premier League, 2022).

Sitting deep, breaking fast

The plan was simple in theory and relentless in execution: sit in two disciplined banks of four, absorb pressure with centre-backs Wes Morgan and Robert Huth close to their own box, and break at pace the moment possession changed hands. Leicester's average possession that season was 42.4% -- comfortably the lowest recorded by any Premier League champion since such data started being tracked in 2003-04.

The engine: N'Golo Kanté

None of it worked without N'Golo Kanté. Signed from Caen for just £5.6m, the Frenchman made 175 tackles and 156 interceptions across the season -- both the most in the division -- screening the back four and winning back the ball that fed Leicester's counters in the first place.

Vardy and Mahrez in transition

Once Leicester won the ball, Jamie Vardy and Riyad Mahrez did the rest. Vardy finished the season with 24 goals and set a Premier League record by scoring in 11 consecutive matches, while Mahrez added 17 goals and 11 assists on his way to the PFA Players' Player of the Year award. Ranieri stayed remarkably consistent with his selection throughout, making just 33 changes to his starting XI all season -- the second-fewest of any title-winning side this century, behind only Manchester United's 1992-93 squad.

A slip at Stamford Bridge

Leicester lost only three league games all season and went unbeaten over their final 12 matches, but the title was ultimately confirmed without them kicking a ball: Tottenham Hotspur, their closest challengers, threw away a two-goal lead at Chelsea in a bad-tempered 2-2 draw remembered as "The Battle of the Bridge," handing Leicester the championship with two games still to play (Yahoo Sports/Football Muse, 2026). Leicester finished the season on 81 points, ten clear of second-placed Arsenal.

Where it led

The years since have not been kind to the class of 2015-16 -- a Champions League run and an FA Cup followed, but Leicester have since been relegated from the Premier League entirely, a decline that stands as a strange coda to the club's greatest achievement. Whatever came after, for one season Ranieri's Leicester proved that a settled squad, a disciplined shape and a willingness to give up the ball could still be enough to out-think the division's biggest spenders.
