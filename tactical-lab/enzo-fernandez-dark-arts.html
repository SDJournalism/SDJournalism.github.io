The Dark Arts

Enzo Fernández arrived at Manchester City on transfer deadline day, training for barely two days before an injury to Nico O'Reilly forced Enzo Maresca into throwing him straight into a Premier League start against Coventry City -- and inside the opening exchanges, away supporters were already jeering a player they'd barely had time to learn the name of (SI.com, 2026). Two weeks and a controversial Manchester derby later, that reception looks like it said more about the opposition than about him.

Booed on arrival, trusted within the hour

The numbers from that first Coventry appearance explain why Maresca stuck with him. Fernández completed 66 of 73 passes -- a 90% completion rate -- put eight passes into the final third, double any other Manchester City player on the pitch that day, and played a line-breaking ball that set up Erling Haaland's winning goal (SI.com, 2026). Maresca, who managed Fernández previously at Chelsea, played down the emergency nature of the selection afterwards: "The reason why Enzo was on the bench was just because he arrived two days ago... I think it was quite good and I'm happy also with his performance" (SI.com, 2026).

The reputation that follows him

None of that composure has softened the other side of his game. Fernández has long carried a reputation, generously described by some as involving the game's "dark arts" -- niggling fouls and a willingness to slow a match down the moment it stops going his way -- and Coventry's travelling fans jeered him through the ninety minutes for exactly that reason (Yahoo Sports, 2026). The same pattern followed him into the Manchester derby a week later.

The calm in the eye of the derby storm

With City reduced to ten men inside 23 minutes at Old Trafford after Phil Foden's red card, Fernández was the player City trusted to keep dictating tempo down a man for over an hour -- and it was his part in the build-up to Haaland's eventual winner that Manchester United spent the rest of the afternoon disputing, insisting the move had strayed offside before the ball reached the Norwegian (Sky Sports, 2026). It's the paradox that already defines his Manchester City career: a player opponents can't stand being on the pitch with, doing exactly the job his manager brought him in to do.
