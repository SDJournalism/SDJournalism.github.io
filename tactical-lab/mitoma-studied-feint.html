The Studied Feint

Most wingers learn to dribble on a training pitch. Kaoru Mitoma wrote about it academically -- while playing for the University of Tsukuba's football club, he delayed turning professional until 22 and submitted his graduation thesis on the mechanics of dribbling. Years later, that research is still visible in every 1v1 he wins for Brighton.

Reading the defender

It starts before Mitoma even touches the ball. He identifies the space he needs to attack, and if there isn't one, he manufactures it with a body feint -- a quick shift of weight designed to make the full-back committed to defending a movement that never actually happens. The point isn't the flashiness of the feint itself; it's reading how the defender reacts and reversing the decision in real time.

The disguised first touch

Once the defender has bitten, Mitoma's first touch does the damage. He directs the ball toward the advantageous space without ever letting it stop -- keeping his head up and the ball close so the defender has no moment to reset. Coaching analysts have highlighted this as the technical core of his game: a guided touch, taken with whichever foot is furthest from the opponent, that beats a full-back before they've registered the ball has moved (Ekkono Coaching, 2023).

Driving inside

From there, Mitoma is direct rather than fussy. He drives the ball at pace into the inner channels whenever a defence fails to close the passing lanes, using the space he's just created to either finish the move himself or set up a team-mate. It's a repeatable pattern rather than a one-off trick -- the same sequence of feint, touch, and drive that has made him one of the most heavily-fouled attackers in the Premier League.

The numbers behind the theory

The results have been hard to ignore. Mitoma's solo goal on the opening weekend of 2023-24, against Wolverhampton Wanderers, won him the Premier League's Goal of the Month award -- the first Japanese player to win it. He returned the favour eighteen months later with another Goal of the Month, this time against Chelsea, which was later voted the BBC's Goal of the Season for 2024-25.

Built to last

A serious back injury ended his 2023-24 season early, but Mitoma signed a new four-year contract with Brighton that October, through to 2027, and returned in 2024-25 to post a career-best 11 Premier League goals. For a player whose entire game is built on repeatable technique rather than raw pace, that durability of method is arguably the most telling stat of all.
