The Rehearsal

Aston Villa were the better side for long spells at Villa Park against Arsenal at the end of August, Emiliano Buendia's early strike rattling the crossbar inside the opening quarter of an hour before Bukayo Saka's second-half finish separated the sides in what finished a 1-0 defeat that flattered the champions (TNT Sports, 2026). It's a pattern that has followed Unai Emery for years now: performances that consistently outstrip the scoreline, built on a level of opposition-specific preparation that has become the clearest signature of his management.

A different plan for every single opponent

Emery's methods start long before kick-off. His weekly analysis is understood to work backwards from the opponent rather than forwards from his own team, identifying two or three moments he expects to be decisive and rehearsing the exact scenario in training so nothing that happens on matchday is a surprise. His captain John McGinn has described it as unlike anything he's encountered in the game: "His drive to succeed, and the hours he puts into the most minute of details to give us the best possible chance to win a game, is something I've never seen before" (Coach Notes, 2026).

Two banks of four, built to strangle the counter

In an attacking 4-2-3-1, Emery drops his number ten alongside the striker and pulls his wide players level with the double pivot out of possession, compressing into two flat banks of four that shuffle across the pitch together rather than press aggressively out of position. It's a patient, central-first structure -- shutting off the middle before worrying about the ball out wide -- and it produced one of the most eye-catching numbers in the Premier League last season: Villa conceded zero goals from opposition counter-attacks across the entire 2025/26 campaign (Total Football Analysis, 2026).

The same idea, adjusted for the opponent in front of him

That structure isn't fixed -- it bends depending on who's in front of it. Villa's passes-per-defensive-action figure, a proxy for pressing intensity, swung from 20.43 away at Liverpool to just 4.99 at home to Wolves last season, evidence that Emery's teams press as hard as the occasion demands and no harder (Total Football Analysis, 2026). It's the same instinct that shapes his recruitment and his team talks: sign players who absorb detail rather than crave autonomy, then ask them to defend a specific plan rather than a general principle. Four years into the job, that plan has Villa competing with sides who spend considerably more than they do -- even on nights, like the one against Arsenal, when the final score says otherwise.
