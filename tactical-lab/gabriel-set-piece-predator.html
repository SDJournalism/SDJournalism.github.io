The Set-Piece Predator

Gabriel Magalhães is a centre-back by trade, but at Arsenal's attacking corners he is treated like a striker. Opposition managers now routinely assign two markers to shadow him, and it still isn't enough -- the Brazilian has scored more Premier League corner goals than any other player since the start of the 2023-24 season, all of them headers.

The delivery

It starts with how Arsenal take their corners. Almost all of them are inswingers -- 96% by one count, whipped in by Bukayo Saka from the right and Declan Rice from the left -- and Arsenal take a lower proportion of short corners than any other Premier League side. When Arsenal win a corner, the ball is going in the box, virtually every time.

Crowding the box

Before the delivery even arrives, Arsenal pack the opposition's six-yard box with more bodies than any other team in the league -- an average of 3.8 players. Those runners force the defending side to match them, which drags markers away from clean sightlines and clutters the goalkeeper's ability to come and claim the ball.

The block

Once the box is crowded, Arsenal's other players go to work as decoys and blockers, screening off Gabriel's marker just long enough for him to lose his man. It's a well-drilled routine rather than chaos -- team-mates like Jurriën Timber, Ben White and William Saliba have all been used specifically to occupy defenders and goalkeepers so Gabriel arrives at the six-yard box unmarked (Opta Analyst, 2024).

The predator's instinct

What the system can't fully explain is Gabriel's own hunger to attack the ball. "When I was young I played up front so I scored a lot of goals," he explained. "It's obviously a lot harder to do that as a defender, but I've worked at it and I've learned a lot. Whenever I go forward for a free-kick or a corner, I know it's a big chance for us. I put that thought in my head: 'I can score this one.' It's my mentality at every single corner." (Goal.com, 2026)

Why it keeps working

Gabriel has also come to see the double-marking as an advantage rather than a problem. "It's getting more and more difficult because when I arrive in the box often there will be two players coming to mark me," he said. "That's really good for us though, because it's not only me who's going to score. It opens up spaces for the others and they can all score too." With defences forced to choose between marking Gabriel and covering everyone else Arsenal send into the box, someone is usually left free.
