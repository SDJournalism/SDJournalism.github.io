The Box Crasher

Across three Premier League seasons under Mauricio Pochettino, Dele Alli built a reputation less as a conventional attacking midfielder than as a support striker who happened to start in midfield. The clearest description of that duality still belongs to Pochettino himself, on Alli after a hard-fought home win over Southampton in 2016/17: "In the box he looks like a striker, and outside the box he plays like a midfielder."

Shuffling to disappear

Alli's game away from the box was built on constant small adjustments -- shuffling between the lines just enough to stay available for a pass without ever giving a marker a fixed reference point to track.

The blindside run

The real damage came from what he did once possession arrived elsewhere. Alli specialised in drifting into a slightly withdrawn position before darting onto the blindside of an opposition centre-back -- attacking the space behind a defender's eyeline rather than in front of it, which is why so many of his box arrivals looked, to defenders, like he'd appeared from nowhere.

Built around Kane, not alongside him

The mechanism depended on Harry Kane. Kane's own habit of dropping short to link play vacated exactly the space Alli was running into, meaning the pair's movement pinned an opposition back line in two directions at once -- one striker dropping short, another arriving late from deep.

The numbers

Across his first three Premier League seasons at Tottenham, only Manchester City's David Silva touched the ball inside the opposition box more often than Alli among Premier League midfielders. His 2016/17 season -- 18 Premier League goals, most of them born from exactly this kind of off-the-ball movement -- won him a second consecutive PFA Young Player of the Year award, a feat only Ryan Giggs, Robbie Fowler and Wayne Rooney had managed before him.
