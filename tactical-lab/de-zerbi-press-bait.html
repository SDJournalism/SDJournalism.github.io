Manager DNA · Roberto De Zerbi · Tottenham Hotspur

The Press-Bait

Roberto De Zerbi was appointed Tottenham head coach on 31 March 2026, arriving with a footballing identity built at Sassuolo, Shakhtar Donetsk and Brighton & Hove Albion long before he took charge in north London. The clearest signature of that identity is what's become known as "press-baiting" -- and it starts with a decision that looks, on first glance, needlessly risky.

The trap, explained

Instead of clearing danger from inside their own box, De Zerbi's centre-backs are coached to do the opposite: keep the ball dead under a boot, in a risky area, until an opponent commits to winning it back. That single committed presser is the target. Once he's fully engaged -- weight forward, momentum carrying him in -- the ball moves, and the space he just vacated becomes the route through him rather than around him.

Why patience is the trigger

During De Zerbi's time at Brighton, his side led the Premier League for passes completed in their own defensive third, and the same pattern has followed him to Tottenham. It isn't nervousness on the ball. Every pass inside that zone is a deliberate invitation, designed to draw an opponent's press out of shape before the team plays through the gap it leaves behind.

Shadow pressing

The mechanism only works because of what a teammate is doing off the ball. While the presser closes down, a Spurs player has already been shadowing his run, staying just out of his cover shadow. The moment the presser commits, that pass goes straight into the space he's vacated -- turning the opponent's own pressure into the thing that beats them.

In the match: Spurs 2-1 Aston Villa

The pattern showed up clearly away at Villa. Richarlison held a position that blocked Villa's central passing lane, forcing the ball wide into a lower-value area, while Rodrigo Bentancur stepped up from midfield to cover the space Richarlison had vacated. It's coordinated pressing in waves rather than chaotic sprinting -- every player's movement tied to what the man beside him is doing.

The numbers

The scale of it shows up in the data: Spurs have been winning the ball back in the final third 5.3 times per game under De Zerbi this season, the highest figure of any team under a single manager in the league (Premier League, 2026) -- a number worth treating as a snapshot from in-season reporting rather than a fully audited season total, but striking either way.
