The Hybrid Press

Liverpool didn't hire Andoni Iraola for continuity. They hired him because Bournemouth, on a fraction of the budget, pressed the Premier League's biggest clubs off the pitch -- and now that same identity is being transplanted to Anfield.

Zonal shape, man-to-man trigger

Iraola's press works in stages rather than a single instruction. Bournemouth held a compact zonal shape through the middle of the pitch, using the forward's run to force the goalkeeper's pass wide -- the moment that flips the system into full man-to-man marking, with wide traps designed to isolate whoever receives it (Total Football Analysis, 2026).

High risk by design

The approach depends on centre-backs following opposing forwards aggressively even when it means stepping out of position, a trade-off Iraola has always defended in public: "The better the opposition, the more risk you have to take if you want to press them... if you are afraid, if you change your identity, it is only a matter of time until they score against you" (Sky Sports, 2026).

The numbers behind it

It isn't just bravado. Bournemouth led the Premier League in PPDA at 8.53 and ball recoveries per 90 at 47.92 last season, generating 10 high-turnover goals -- while somehow conceding the fewest fast-break shots in the entire division. Pressing this aggressively is supposed to leave space in behind; Iraola's version consistently didn't (Total Football Analysis, 2026).

Arriving at Anfield

Liverpool's own squad is built for exactly this kind of work rate, and Iraola has already been candid about the adjustment: "For me, as coach, it was a change arriving in the Premier League... here, I would say it's more transitional" (Sky Sports, 2026). Whether a Bournemouth-sized press can survive a full season of Champions League football and a squad accustomed to a more controlled possession game is the question Anfield spends 2026/27 finding the answer to.
