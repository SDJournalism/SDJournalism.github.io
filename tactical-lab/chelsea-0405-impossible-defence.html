The Impossible Defence

No Premier League team has conceded fewer than 15 goals in a season since -- and two decades on, nobody's got especially close. Jose Mourinho's first Chelsea side didn't just win the title in 2004/05; they redefined what a defence could look like.

The switch to 4-3-3

Mourinho's first major decision was structural: moving Chelsea from a 4-4-2 into a 4-3-3 built around Claude Makelele screening the back four. It gave John Terry and Ricardo Carvalho a permanent extra body in front of them and let the team defend a full pitch higher up without ever losing central control (Premier League, 2026).

Winning 1-0 became the plan

Chelsea lost only once all season, dropped points from a 1-0 lead just a single time, and strung together ten consecutive clean sheets between December and February. Petr Cech later put it simply: "We suddenly realised that winning 1-0 gives you a chance to win the title" -- and a defence built around Makelele made that outcome the default, not the exception (beIN Sports, 2026).

Team-wide, not just the back five

What made the record remarkable wasn't only individual quality, though Cech's 87.3 percent save percentage was extraordinary in its own right. It was collective: "It's not only about the five defenders and the goalkeeper, it is about the team, how well you help each other," Cech explained -- with Frank Lampard's box-to-box running and Arjen Robben and Damien Duff's willingness to track back turning the whole side into a defensive unit (beIN Sports, 2026).

A record that keeps surviving

Twenty years on, the record still stands: 15 goals conceded and 25 clean sheets from a single season (Premier League, 2026). Arsenal's own record-low defence last season came the closest of the last decade, but even they had shipped 17 goals inside 24 matches -- almost as many as Chelsea allowed in an entire year (beIN Sports, 2026).
