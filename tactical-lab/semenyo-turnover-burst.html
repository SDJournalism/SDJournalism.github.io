The Turnover Burst

Antoine Semenyo's rise from a breakout season on the south coast to a £64m Manchester City signing took less than eighteen months, but the movement pattern behind it barely needed to change. Whether he was wearing Bournemouth's red and black or Manchester City's sky blue, the same sequence recurs: an opponent loses the ball in a dangerous area, and Semenyo is already sprinting before most of the crowd has processed the turnover.

The trigger

It starts with what he does without the ball. Under Andoni Iraola at Bournemouth, Semenyo regularly ranked among the Premier League's best forwards for regaining possession in the final third -- pressing not for effort's sake, but to manufacture exactly the kind of broken-play moment his pace and power were built to exploit.

The sprint

Once the ball is loose, Semenyo doesn't wait to receive it into feet -- he runs in behind first and lets the pass find him. That directness made him one of the most heavily targeted outlets in Bournemouth's team for progressive passes, and it's the same instinct that has made him a natural transition partner for Erling Haaland at Manchester City: one occupies the last defender centrally, the other attacks the space that decision creates.

The move to City

Manchester City beat off competition from Liverpool and Tottenham to sign Semenyo from Bournemouth in January 2026 (Sky Sports, 2026), and the Ghana international didn't take long to get off the mark for his new club, scoring the opening goal in a League Cup semi-final first-leg win away at Newcastle.

The numbers

The form that prompted the move was hard to ignore: at the point of his exit, Semenyo had contributed to roughly 29% of Bournemouth's Premier League goals in 2025/26, sitting third in the division for goals scored behind only Haaland and Igor Thiago -- remarkable output for a player whose game is built as much around what he does before he gets the ball as what he does with it.
