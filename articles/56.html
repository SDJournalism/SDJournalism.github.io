Scouting Report: Luca Williams-Barnett, the Spurs Teenager De Zerbi Compares to Lamine Yamal

Tottenham have a new academy star. Luca Williams-Barnett, 18, scored on his first Premier League start in Saturday's 2-0 win over Ipswich and helped set up Dominic Solanke's second goal (Sky Sports, 2026). Afterwards, Roberto De Zerbi was asked whether the teenager could reach the level of Barcelona's Lamine Yamal. His answer: "Yes, I think so" (Sky Sports, 2026).

Profile

Born on 1 October 2008 (ESPN, 2026), Williams-Barnett played for his hometown club Luton Town before joining Tottenham in the Under-10s (Tottenham Hotspur, 2026). He is 175cm tall, two-footed and wears the No 68 shirt (Sofascore, 2026).

He became Tottenham's 900th men's first-team player when he made his debut in a Carabao Cup win over Doncaster Rovers in September 2025, and signed his first professional contract after turning 17 that October (Goal, 2026). Before Saturday, his Premier League experience was a handful of appearances off the bench, including the win over Crystal Palace and the defeat at Leeds (Sky Sports, 2026).

The Ipswich breakthrough

Starting on the left wing, he opened the scoring after 15 minutes (Sky Sports, 2026), tucking away Pedro Porro's cross (Soccer News, 2026). Early in the second half his dribble and low cross led to Solanke's goal, although Ipswich goalkeeper Christian Walton's touch on the ball meant he was not officially credited with the assist (Sky Sports, 2026). Solanke had no doubt: "Luca was magnificent today" (Sky Sports, 2026).

"Luca is a special player. I'm not surprised because his talent is bigger than you think," said De Zerbi, who revealed the teenager had been watching YouTube clips of Ronaldinho, Eden Hazard and Neymar on the team bus before the game (Sky Sports, 2026).

"I'm not surprised because his talent is bigger than you think."

Strengths

Goals are the first thing that stands out. Williams-Barnett has scored 30 goals and made 20 assists in 41 official games from Under-16 to Under-21 level, including 19 goals and nine assists in 20 Under-18 Premier League matches (Goal, 2026). He won the Under-18 Premier League goal of the season for a long-range strike against Leicester (Sky Sports, 2026), and has scored from the halfway line three times for Spurs' youth teams (Goal, 2026).

His dribbling is just as dangerous. He accelerates quickly from a standing start and switches the ball between his feet mid-run, which makes him very hard to defend against (Goal, 2026). At the 2025 Under-17 World Cup, he led the whole tournament for dribbling, with 17 successful dribbles in five matches (Yahoo Sports, 2025).

He is versatile too. He mostly played as a No 10 in youth football but has also played on both flanks (Goal, 2026), and he has represented England at every age group from Under-15 to Under-19 (Sky Sports, 2026).

Areas to improve

The big question is the physical side of the senior game. After his debut, he admitted he needed to adjust to the speed and physicality of men's football: "I need to get used to that" (Goal, 2026). Spurs' Under-21 coach Wayne Burnett has said: "He has to keep working. He has to keep developing, but he can be very, very exciting at times" (Goal, 2026).

His work without the ball is the other area to watch. De Zerbi said he asked him to work hard out of possession "like Raphinha, like [Lamine] Yamal", and paired him with James Maddison so the teenager could play with freedom (Sky Sports, 2026).

Player comparison

His hero is Paul Gascoigne, the reason his family supported Spurs: "He just did what he wanted; that's what I like about him" (Goal, 2026). Goal compares his style to Gascoigne and Dele Alli, while noting he is more explosive on the dribble than Alli was in his prime, and has even asked whether he could be Tottenham's best homegrown product since Harry Kane (Goal, 2026).

The verdict

One start is a small sample, and De Zerbi will need to manage his minutes carefully. But the youth numbers, the two-footed dribbling and the end product are all there. If he can handle the physical demands of the Premier League, Tottenham may have found their most exciting academy attacker in years.
