Crystal Palace Sack Pierre Sage: Five Months, 16 Games and the Thomas Frank Gamble

Crystal Palace have sacked Pierre Sage after just five months in charge, with the club bottom of the Premier League after four straight league defeats (BBC Sport, 2026). "Crystal Palace Football Club can confirm that Pierre Sage has left his role as manager," the club said on Thursday, shortly after Manchester United announced Michael Carrick's sacking (Yahoo Sports, 2026).

How it went wrong

The numbers are stark. Palace have won just two of their 10 league games, have the worst defence in the division with 24 goals conceded, and sit 21st in the Europa League with one win from four (BBC Sport, 2026). Across all competitions, Sage's record reads 16 games, five wins, three draws and eight defeats, with three of his coaching staff leaving alongside him (BBC Sport, 2026).

The pressure had been building since a 5-0 defeat to rivals Brighton in October. Asked afterwards whether he would keep his job, Sage simply replied: "I don't know" (The Hard Tackle, 2026). Last weekend's 3-1 home defeat to Liverpool then made a change during the international break inevitable (The Telegraph, 2026).

There were signs of tension behind the scenes too. Sage's decision to drop Canvot was reported to have cut across the board's transfer strategy, and he was defiant when asked about it: "I do not care about my authority. I just want the players at 100 per cent of what they are able to do and when they give less, I have to shake them" (Football Whispers, 2026).

"I do not care about my authority."

A hard act to follow

In fairness to Sage, he walked into a difficult job. He replaced Oliver Glasner, who won the FA Cup, the Community Shield and the Conference League in two and a half years in south London before leaving at the end of his contract and joining Nottingham Forest (Yahoo Sports, 2026). Sage arrived with big credentials, having taken Lens to second place in Ligue 1 behind Paris Saint-Germain (The Telegraph, 2026), but the Premier League proved a very different test.

Why Thomas Frank

Palace already know who they want. Thomas Frank, 53, has been out of work since leaving Tottenham in February with Spurs 16th in the table (The Telegraph, 2026), and he was on Palace's shortlist in the summer before they chose Sage (Evening Standard, 2026). The club also interviewed survival specialist Vitor Pereira, and had considered Sean Dyche last month (The Telegraph, 2026).

Frank's appeal is his Brentford record: he won promotion to the Premier League, kept them there and had them challenging for Europe on one of the smallest budgets in the division (Evening Standard, 2026). As of Sunday, though, the deal was still not confirmed. Talks over his backroom staff and the bonuses in his contract have caused delays, because Frank is joining on significantly less money than he earned at Tottenham (RoundTable, 2026). His long-time staff Justin Cochrane, Joe Newton and Chris Haslam are expected to follow him (RoundTable, 2026).

Two big games waiting

Frank is likely to be in charge for next weekend's trip to Coventry (Evening Standard, 2026), with a game against Hull City to follow (RoundTable, 2026). He knows how to build a club's confidence from his Brentford days. Whether he can do it ten games into a relegation fight, after his own reputation took a knock at Spurs, could decide Palace's season.
