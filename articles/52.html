Manchester United Sack Michael Carrick: From Permanent Deal to the Exit in 173 Days

Manchester United have sacked Michael Carrick, confirming on Thursday that he "has departed his role as men's first-team head coach" with the club 18th in the Premier League and only one league win from ten games (ESPN, 2026). He goes just 173 days after signing a two-year deal to become the permanent head coach (Evening Standard, 2026).

How the decision was made

The process played out over several days. United's board met on Wednesday, when the recommendation was made that Carrick be relieved of his duties (LiveScore, 2026). Omar Berrada and director of football Jason Wilcox met minority owner Sir Jim Ratcliffe in Monaco that day, then met Carrick at Carrington on Thursday (ESPN, 2026).

The club's statement said that after a "disappointing start to the season" the club has come to the extremely difficult conclusion that a change is necessary (ESPN, 2026). Sources at United told ESPN that being 18th after ten games was "not acceptable" and that the squad is "far better than results and performances so far this season have indicated" (ESPN, 2026).

"The club has come to the extremely difficult conclusion that a change is necessary."

From saviour to the sack

What makes this so striking is how quickly it fell apart. Carrick took over as interim head coach in January after Ruben Amorim was dismissed, won his first game 2-0 against Manchester City and went on to win 12 of 17 matches to finish third, earning a permanent contract in May (ESPN, 2026). The club's own statement pointed out that United collected the highest points tally in the league during that run (Evening Standard, 2026).

This season has been the opposite. United have won three of 15 games in all competitions, and the only victories came against Ipswich Town, Sabah and Roma (All Out Soccer, 2026). The defeats include a 2-0 loss to newly promoted Hull City, a Carabao Cup exit to Brighton after leading 2-0, a 4-1 Champions League defeat to Como and a 3-0 home loss to Bournemouth (ESPN, 2026).

His last game summed up the problem. United came back from two goals down twice to draw 4-4 with Aston Villa on Sunday, but Carrick admitted afterwards that his side were giving away "totally avoidable goals" (Sky Sports, 2026). The board did not see enough in that draw, the 2-0 defeat at Chelsea or the 3-2 win over Roma to believe the team was heading in the right direction (All Out Soccer, 2026).

A messy week for a club legend

Plenty of people have criticised how United handled it. The sacking dragged on through the week while the club negotiated with his likely replacement, and Carrick kept taking training throughout, with sources inside the club praising his professionalism (All Out Soccer, 2026). United insist they followed "due process" (All Out Soccer, 2026), but ESPN pundit Steve Nicol said the club were showing Carrick no respect by holding talks with Pochettino while he was still in the job (ESPN, 2026).

The statement still called Carrick "a Manchester United legend" (ESPN, 2026), and that is how most supporters will remember a man who won the Premier League and the Champions League in 12 years at Old Trafford as a player (Malay Mail, 2026). Back in February, while still the interim boss, he said: "I want the club to be successful beyond the end of the season. If that's me, if that's somebody else at this stage, I can't control that" (Malay Mail, 2026).

Pochettino waiting in the wings

United say they want a permanent successor "as quickly as possible" (ESPN, 2026), and USA head coach Mauricio Pochettino is the clear favourite. Sources told ESPN that United have informed US Soccer they intend to trigger the $10 million release clause in his contract once personal terms are agreed (ESPN, 2026). He is expected to take charge of the USA's two Concacaf Nations League quarter-final games against Haiti on 14 and 17 November first (ESPN, 2026), and Goal reports he could be in charge for United's next match, away at Liverpool on 22 November (Goal, 2026).

The bigger problem at Old Trafford

Whoever comes in, this is United's third mid-season managerial change in three seasons, after Erik ten Hag in October 2024 and Amorim in January 2026 (ESPN, 2026). United spent around £150m on midfielders this summer without strengthening other areas of the squad (All Out Soccer, 2026), and they have conceded more goals in all competitions than any other side in Europe's major leagues (Sky Sports, 2026). Carrick has paid the price for this start, but the problems in the squad will still be waiting for whoever replaces him.
